Apartment Maintenance UX Case Study

Apartment maintenance reporting, tracking & accountability

Design-thinking case study · Persona + Problem Statement

01 The situation

In apartment communities, maintenance problems are often reported through a mix of WhatsApp messages, phone calls, security staff, maintenance workers, and face-to-face conversations.

The issue is not simply that residents cannot report a problem. The deeper issue is that there is often no single, reliable flow for capturing the problem, deciding its priority, assigning responsibility, tracking progress, and communicating the outcome.

The result: residents follow up repeatedly, maintenance teams receive incomplete or duplicate information, and management has limited visibility into unresolved or recurring issues.

03 Primary persona

Aarav Mehta

32 · Software Engineer · Apartment Resident

“I don't mind reporting the problem. I hate having to chase someone to find out what happened to it.”

About Aarav

Aarav lives in a mid-sized apartment community and works long hours. He is comfortable with technology, but he wants routine household issues to require as little coordination as possible.

Needs

Clarity Visibility Ownership Timely updates

Goals

  • Get maintenance issues acknowledged quickly
  • Know who is handling the issue
  • See when the issue is expected to be resolved
  • Avoid repeated calls and messages

Frustrations

  • Complaints disappear inside busy WhatsApp groups
  • He does not know whether a request was seen or assigned
  • He has to repeat the same information to different people
  • Urgent problems can feel no different from minor requests

Behaviours

  • Takes a photo when the problem is visible
  • Usually reports through the channel that feels fastest
  • Follows up when there is no update
  • Stops trusting the process after repeated delays

06 Pain points

📱 Fragmented reporting

Different residents use different channels, so requests are difficult to consolidate.

🔁 Duplicate complaints

The same broken lift or leaking pipe may be reported by several residents, creating noise instead of clarity.

⏰ Weak prioritization

Urgent issues and cosmetic issues may enter the same communication stream without a consistent priority signal.

👤 Missing ownership

A complaint can exist without a clearly visible person or team responsible for the next action.

👀 No status visibility

Residents often cannot tell whether an issue is new, acknowledged, assigned, in progress, or resolved.

📊 No usable history

Recurring problems are difficult to spot when there is no structured record of previous incidents.

07 Root of the problem

The visible complaint is “my maintenance issue is taking too long.”

The underlying system problem is:

Scattered communication

  • incomplete / duplicate information
  • unclear priority
  • unclear ownership
  • weak status visibility
  • repeated follow-ups
  • frustration for everyone
Core insight: The communication process is fragmented, not merely the complaint form.

08 Problem statement

Primary statement

Apartment residents struggle to get maintenance issues resolved efficiently because complaints are reported through fragmented communication channels, making it difficult to track requests, identify responsibility, and receive timely updates.

USER

Apartment residents

NEED

A clear and reliable way to report, track, and understand the progress of maintenance issues

BECAUSE

Current communication is scattered across multiple channels and often lacks visible ownership, priority, and status

09 Design opportunity

How might we…

How might we make apartment maintenance issues easier to report, easier to prioritize, and easier for residents to follow from report to resolution?

Important: This is an opportunity area, not the solution yet.

10 What should be validated?

This persona and problem statement are working hypotheses until real users are interviewed.

Ask residents

  • How do you currently report maintenance problems?
  • What happens after you report one?
  • How do you know whether it has been assigned?
  • How often do you follow up?
  • Have you ever reported the same issue more than once?

Ask maintenance staff

  • Where do requests come from?
  • Which details are usually missing?
  • How do you decide what to handle first?
  • How often do multiple people report the same issue?

Ask management

  • How are complaints currently tracked?
  • Which issues become overdue most often?
  • Can you identify recurring problems?
  • How do you know whether a complaint is actually resolved?

11 Success looks like

A better process should make it easier to answer five simple questions:

  • What is the problem? → Clear description + evidence
  • Where is it? → Exact location
  • Who owns it? → Responsible person/team
  • What happens next? → Visible status / next action
  • When will it be resolved? → Expected timeline or update

💡 Why this is a strong design-thinking problem

It involves multiple users, competing priorities, repeated communication, uncertainty, and a real service journey. That gives you room to demonstrate observation, persona creation, pain-point analysis, problem framing, and eventually solution exploration without jumping straight to "build an app."

🧠 One-line takeaway

The problem is not reporting a maintenance issue. The problem is the lack of a trustworthy system between reporting the issue and knowing that it is actually being handled.

12 What we know & what we Don’t know

What We Know What We Don’t Know
Residents use multiple ways to report maintenance issues. Which reporting method do residents prefer most?
Complaints can be difficult to track after being reported. How often do complaints get missed or forgotten?
Residents may not receive regular updates on their complaints. How frequently do residents expect status updates?
Some maintenance issues take longer than expected to resolve. What causes the biggest delays in resolution?
Multiple residents can report the same issue. How does management currently handle duplicate complaints?
Maintenance staff receive requests from different sources. How do staff decide which complaints to handle first?
Residents often need to follow up to know the status of an issue. What would make residents feel that their complaint is being properly handled?

Design Thinking Concepts Applied

In this UX case study and specifically within the problem statement formulation, the primary Design Thinking phases utilized are Empathize and Define.

1. Empathize

We demonstrated this phase by creating the Persona (Aarav Mehta) and mapping out the resident's journey (What Aarav experiences today). By identifying the frustrations, behaviors, and needs of all stakeholders (residents, maintenance staff, and management), we deeply understood the user pain points without prematurely jumping to solutions.

2. Define

We synthesized the empathic research to clearly articulate the core issue (Section 07: Root of the Problem). By turning "my maintenance is taking too long" into a structured Problem Statement (Section 08) and framing the challenge with "How might we..." questions (Section 09), we successfully set a clear, actionable direction for the upcoming Ideation phase.
